Early Revision Macintosh 128K

Now that I have the entire Macintosh's casing and chasis cleaned, I think it is a good time to get started on the electrical repairs. Unlike the three Mime 2A terminals that I had previously worked on, the Macintosh 128k/512k models both have their schematics readily available online. Since both the logic board and the analog boards have been scanned and uploaded to the internet and I will be relying on them heavily.

The first thing I will do is remove the rusted components from the board. The rear IO ports, FDD connector, single wipe sockets, and the rusted resistors and capacitors will be the first things desoldered from the motherboard. Here is what it looked like after I had gotten all of these removed from the motherboard.

I referenced the schematics and have replaced the resistors with their correct values on the motherboard making sure to check board revision version to avoid installing the wrong resistor values. I've installed the DB9 and the DB15 connectors that are used for the mouse, serial, and floppy drive connector and also installed machine holed IC sockets for better reliability since I found them for a very good price at Axe Man in Minneapolis. Here is what the new motherboard looks like.
